A field guide for first-time defenders

Garden Defense

A squad of plant heroes guards one garden gnome against ten waves of zombies, then runs for the rescue.

  • Gold is the garden's side: plants, the gnome, you
  • Coral is the dead: zombies, agents, damage

A third-person shooter that runs in a web browser. Third-person means you see your plant from just behind its shoulder, like a camera following an actor. It's inspired by the Garden Ops mode of Plants vs. Zombies: Garden Warfare 2.

№01

Packet 01 of 08 · The goal

Guard the gnome

Everything on the map is walking toward one statue. Keep it standing.

In the middle of a 440 × 440 metre garden stands a painted gnome on a stone plinth. That's about four football pitches laid end to end, in both directions.

The gnome has 1,000 health points. Health points (HP) are a simple counter: every hit takes some away. Zombies walk in from the edges and beat on the gnome. If its counter reaches zero, the match is lost.

The gnome is never repaired. Damage from wave 1 is still there in wave 10, so every hit it takes now is a hit you'll miss later. You can see the damage, too: the paint grimes, and below half health its hat tip snaps off.

Zombies appear in four areas. They don't follow fixed lanes. Each one plans its own route, so pressure can come from any side.

  • 1,000

    HP for the gnome, for the whole match.

  • 100

    HP lost if one Bomber zombie reaches the gnome. That's a tenth of its life in one blast.

  • 2 ways

    to lose: the gnome hits zero, or the squad misses the rescue at the end (chapter 8).

Packet 02 of 08 · The heroes

Choose your squad

Seven plant heroes. You play one, and up to three more fight beside you on their own.

Before the match you pick your plant plus zero to three companions. The computer plays the companions, and each kind has its own habits. The Sunflower sticks to wounded friends, the Cactus guards spots near the gnome, and the Kernel Corn roams out to hunt bosses.

Every plant has a main weapon on the left mouse button and three abilities on Q, E and F. An ability is a special move with a cooldown: a recharge wait before you can use it again. Press Tab or 1–4 to jump into a different plant mid-fight. The one you leave keeps fighting by itself.

Packet 04 of 08 · Who wants what

Two kinds of enemy

The horde wants the gnome. The agents want you.

The horde

Many, simple, and aimed at the gnome.

  • 761 zombies across the ten waves on Normal with one plant, and more with a bigger squad.
  • Each plans its own route. Busy paths and jams push others onto different paths.
  • Zombies far from both the gnome and every plant walk 1.75× faster, so quiet stretches stay short.
  • The last six of a wave hurry in too, so a wave doesn't drag on.
vs

Tactical agents

Few, clever, and aimed at plants.

  • Seven zombie heroes with weapons and abilities like yours: Imp, Foot Soldier, All-Star, Captain Deadbeard, Super Brainz, Engineer and Scientist.
  • They join packs as escorts. The first two can come from wave 1, and one more type unlocks each wave up to wave 6.
  • They never retreat or give up on the plants.
  • They wait for a weak moment, called an opening, then rush in together.

Try it: what an agent sees

Agents score every plant for weakness. Tick what's true for your plant and watch the score. At 2 or more, agents in range stop poking from a distance and charge.

Packet 05 of 08 · The pressure

Ten waves, rising

Each wave is bigger than the last, and new kinds of zombie join in. Move the slider and watch the horde grow.

Zombies arrive in packs: groups that appear together, a few seconds apart. Bring friends and the horde grows to match. Every plant beyond the first adds 40% to each pack. Difficulty changes the count too, along with how tough the zombies are and how hard they hit. Between waves there's a 5-second countdown and a short summary. There's no shop and no repairs.

    Packet 07 of 08 · Teamwork

    Falling isn’t failing

    Plants go down all the time. They come back.

    Down

    At 0 HP a plant falls over. It isn't gone yet, but the horde keeps walking to the gnome.

    →
    Revive

    A friend holds X beside it for 5 s (a Sunflower needs only 3). It gets back up with half its health.

    or
    Respawn

    Nobody came? After 20 s it pops back in beside the gnome, fully healed.

    Even if the whole squad is down at once, the match goes on. But while nobody is standing, nothing stops the horde from reaching the gnome. Reviving is faster than waiting, which makes staying close to friends a real tactic.

    Your computer-played friends watch the gnome too. When a zombie starts hitting it, plants within 40 m drop what they're doing and deal with it. Plants also heal slowly on their own once they've been out of danger for a few seconds.

    Packet 08 of 08 · The finale

    Run for the rescue

    Clear wave 10 and a landing zone opens somewhere out in the map. Hold it until the rescue arrives.

    The zone is a circle 18 m across, between 70 and 115 m from the gnome. It charges only while a living plant stands inside. Alone, that takes 120 seconds. Each extra plant inside makes it charge 35% faster.

    You have 240 seconds. If the zone isn't full by then, the rescue leaves without you, and that's a defeat.

    The last horde changes its mind. It ignores the gnome and hunts plants: a group of 3 to 6 zombies every 4 seconds, up to 50 at once, sometimes with an agent along.

    Try it: hold the zone

    1

    One plant inside: the zone fills in 120 s, with 120 s to spare before the deadline.

    The clock runs 12× faster than the game. You can move the slider while it runs.

    Plants grow back. Gnomes don’t.

    Ten waves, one gnome, up to four plants. Everything else is about keeping that one statue standing.

    Four difficulties

    Casual, Normal, Hard and Crazy. Crazy gives zombies 1.7× health and damage and sends 1.45× as many.

    Mastery

    Each plant saves its own XP across matches: ten levels and titles from Sprout to Master. It's for bragging rights, never extra power.

    Watch squad

    A button under the start button lets every plant play itself while you watch through their eyes, orbit them, or fly freely.

    Made of code

    No 3D models, textures or sound files. Every shape, sound and song is generated in the browser by the game’s own code.
